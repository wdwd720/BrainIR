Scientists can map every connection in a fly's nervous system and simulate it, to learn which neurons control behaviour. We
checked how reproducible one simulation's list of walking neurons is, time-stamping our tests in advance. The simulation often disagreed
with itself about a neuron's left and right copies, and about the same neuron in different flies. A third fly, screened only after our
rules were fixed, showed the same, though its wiring map also differed more from the other two. How many top neurons recurred depended
on how the simulation was run. The two neurons the original authors confirmed in real flies behaved as they reported. In the one usable
test against real experiments, counting connections did as well.
