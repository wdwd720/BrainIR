Scientists can now map every connection in a fly's nervous system and simulate it, hoping to learn which neurons control behaviour. We
checked how reproducible one simulation's list of walking neurons is, time-stamping our tests in advance. The simulation often
disagreed with itself about a neuron's left and right copies, and about the same neuron in different flies. A third fly, examined only
after our rules were fixed, confirmed this, though its wiring map also differed more from the other two. The two neurons the original
authors confirmed in real flies behaved in our hands as they reported. The simulation predicted real experiments no better than counting
connections. We have listed twelve experiments that would show which predictions to trust.
