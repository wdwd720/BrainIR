Scientists can now map every connection in a fly's nervous system and simulate it, hoping the simulation will show which neurons control
behaviour. We checked how far one such simulation, of walking, can be trusted, writing down and time-stamping every test before it ran.
The simulation often disagreed with itself about a neuron's left and right copies, and about the same neuron in a second fly; tiny
random differences in wiring were enough to cause that. Its predictions matched real experiments no better than counting connections.
But the two neurons its authors had confirmed in real flies held up in the second fly. We have listed twelve experiments that would
show which kind of prediction to trust.
