Scientists can now map every connection in a fly's nervous system and simulate it, hoping to learn which neurons control behaviour. We
checked how far one simulation, of walking, can be trusted, time-stamping most tests in advance. The simulation often disagreed with
itself about a neuron's left and right copies, and about the same neuron in a second fly; tiny random wiring differences can produce the
left-right disagreement. Its predictions matched real experiments no better than counting connections. Looking back (post hoc), the two
neurons its authors had confirmed in real flies held up in the second fly, though our method guaranteed one of them. We have listed
twelve experiments that would show which kind of prediction to trust.
