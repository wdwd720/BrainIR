# Calibration targets for the synthetic causal-state suite (goal5 section 8)

Generic summary statistics of ten simulated real systems, computed from PUBLIC trajectories built with the benchmark's OWN dataset design (version 2: no explicit initial states; LOG P4-D36), the same generic protocol design (passive sets, reference interventions with counterfactual twins, validation and test items, pool sources) that builds the synthetic tiers. A synthetic suite built by the same design is therefore directly comparable, including on the statistics that depend on the design. The suite should be distributionally realistic: its systems should span these ranges, not copy any single system.

Machine-readable targets: `benchmarks/causal_state_v1/public/calibration_targets.json` (version 3), built by `scripts/p4/calibration_targets.py` from the public real data with the documented rules below; this document is generated by `scripts/p4/calibration_targets_doc.py`. The statistics are computed by `brainir_causal.calibstats` (`load_dataset_dir`, `compute_all`; `compare` checks a suite against the targets for acceptance criterion 11; `dependence` says whether a statistic is a system property or depends on the design). Earlier versions: `calibration_targets_v2_explicit_init_design.json` (design with explicit initial states) and `calibration_targets_v1_earlier_design.json`.

## The data design behind these numbers

- records per system: large systems 521 (40 passive + 120 reference interventions + their 120 twins + 9 passive and 24 intervention validation records + 24 twins + 24 test interventions + 24 twins + 16 passive tests + 120 pool sources); small systems 713 (the same with 200 reference and 40 validation interventions)
- intervention kinds: single-target kicks, current pulses and temporary silencing (no persistent silencing)
- magnitude classes: below / weak / moderate / strong = 0.1 / 0.3 / 1 / 3 x a moderate magnitude per kind, jittered by 0.8-1.25 (strong never above 3 x); silencing has no magnitude; a kick clipped at the floor is labelled by its realized size
- onsets: uniform in [0.15, 0.5] of the trajectory duration
- initial states: no explicit initial states (setting a unit's initial value is a kick at t = 0): initial-condition variability comes from 'obs.init' restarts at 25-90 % of a nominal passive trajectory of the same parameter draw; the pools' 'init' sources restart from the first (nominal) source of their draw
- input levels: 0.55-1.45 of the nominal input, including multi-step schedules
- structural noise: multiplicative weight noise with sd up to 0.1
- twins: every intervention record has a counterfactual twin with the same parameters, initial condition (rest or restart source) and input and no event; twins keep their item's integration pieces (no-op input breakpoints at the event times)
- pulse durations: 19-150 ms (5th-95th percentile)
- record selection: every public record of each system (calibstats.load_dataset_dir without a cap): 6554 records, 521-713 per system; per-trajectory statistics use at most 40 records per record kind inside compute_all

## The real systems in brief

System properties (stable across intervention designs):

- **Two regimes.** Three large recurrent networks (197-213 observed units, 9-20 readout units, one input) and seven small sub-circuits of them (3-6 observed units, the same readout and input). Output sampling 1 ms, trajectories 2 s.
- **Oscillation.** Every system oscillates, with dominant frequencies of 6.8-17 Hz and 95 % of the spectral power below 29 Hz; decorrelation within 11-29 ms; responses to the input onset start within 23-55 ms.
- **Rectified, sparse activity.** Active units of the large systems sit at the floor for 52-59 % of samples, those of the small ones for 7.5-9.3 %. Part of the readout is silent in the small systems (45-80 % of readout units active).
- **Low-dimensional trajectories.** One trajectory has a participation ratio of 1.1-2.3 (2-3 components for 95 % of its variance); the readout's participation ratio is 1.0-1.4.
- **Relaxation.** Transient intervention effects decay to 1/e within 22-76 ms after the event ends.

Properties revealed by the design, or scaled by it:

- **Initial conditions and persistent states.** Restarts at 25-90 % of nominal trajectories leave the large systems in their nominal regime: their late activity is 0.97-1.0 x the nominal (readout 0.83-1.0 x), and 0 % of the restarts end in a persistent high-activity state (0-11 % in the small systems). The explicit initial states of version 2 had moved two of the three large systems into such a state in about 70 % of the records and thereby set their rate scales; the scales now come from nominal activity (s_x 12-15, s_y 4.0-13 in the large systems).
- **Condition diversity.** Pooled over parameter draws, input schedules and restarts the large systems need 9-11 components for 95 % of the variance (small systems 1-2). Parameter draws change trajectories substantially (spread 0.19-1.1 of the mean level; 1.2-4.6 x the dynamic range of the draw-averaged trajectory, because draws dephase the oscillation).
- **Interventions reveal more than passive activity.** Pooled intervention effects need 17-25 components for 95 % of their energy in the large systems, and 0.43-65 % of their effect energy lies OUTSIDE the subspace that holds 95 % of the passive variance (47-65 % in the large systems, 0.43-27 % in the small ones).
- **Effect sizes under this design.** In the large systems most interventions are invisible at the readout: 61-69 % change it by less than 1 % of its own variability, and a single-target intervention moves only 7.2-10 % of the other observed units by more than 1 % of the scale. In the small systems 77-99 % of the other units respond. Median effects relative to the counterfactual twin's variability: x 0.014-0.016 (large), 0.59-7.0 (small); readout 0.0016-0.0031 (large), 0.51-6.7 (small). Relative to the spread across parameter draws, readout effects are 0.0013-0.0022 (large) and 0.19-0.84 (small).
- **Delayed consequences.** Readout effects peak 54-560 ms after the intervention onset, and part of the effect persists to the end of the trajectory (1.0-39 % of its peak).
- **Clipping.** 20-51 % of all kick offsets are clipped at the floor (negative kicks on units at or near rest); such a kick can have no effect at all. A clipped kick item is labelled by its realized size.
- **Input nonlinearity.** Activity grows as the 1.8-6.4th power of the input scale over the input levels of the design (readout 2.8-6.8).
- **Noise.** The simulations are deterministic and twins share their item's integration pieces, so the difference before an event is exactly 0; the only floor is the float32 storage resolution. Observation noise is a benchmark parameter.

## Design dependence: system properties and design-dependent statistics

- **system**: a property of the system's own dynamics under nominal conditions; the intervention design changes it little
- **design**: set mainly by the experimental design (magnitudes, onsets, durations, initial conditions, input schedules, record mix); comparable only between data built with the same design
- **mixed**: a system response whose value also scales with the design

The `change` columns compare every statistic with version 2 of the targets (the same systems under the design with explicit initial states): exp(median over systems of |log(current / earlier)|), with the largest per-system change in brackets; 'N from 0' / 'N to 0' count systems where the earlier / current value is 0.

## Definitions of the checked statistics

- `acf_decay_s_median`: median over active units and event-free trajectories of the lag (s) at which the autocorrelation first drops below 1/e (window after the onset transient); uncensored units only. For oscillating units this is about a quarter period, not a memory time
- `decay_x_s_median`: time (s) from the peak of ||x_int - x_twin|| after the end of a transient first event to its fall below peak / e; median over pairs
- `dim_effect_x_n95`: PCA (uncentred) of the intervention effects x_int - x_twin pooled over all (intervention, twin) pairs and post-onset samples: pr / n90 / n95 / n99
- `dim_effect_x_pr`: PCA (uncentred) of the intervention effects x_int - x_twin pooled over all (intervention, twin) pairs and post-onset samples: pr / n90 / n95 / n99
- `dim_x_pooled_obs_n95`: PCA of all event-free trajectories pooled (sub-sampled every 5 ms): pr / n90 / n95 / n99
- `dim_x_traj_n95`: PCA of one trajectory's x (covariance, centred): participation ratio (pr) or number of components for 90/95/99 % variance (n90/n95/n99); median over trajectories (up to 40 per record kind)
- `dim_x_traj_pr`: PCA of one trajectory's x (covariance, centred): participation ratio (pr) or number of components for 90/95/99 % variance (n90/n95/n99); median over trajectories (up to 40 per record kind)
- `dim_y_traj_pr`: the same for the readout y of one trajectory
- `dt`: output sampling interval (s)
- `eff_rel_x_p50`: intervention effect in x: ||x_int - x_twin||_F / ||x_twin - mean_t x_twin||_F over the window from the first event to the end (effect relative to the twin's own variability); percentiles over pairs; suffix = kind of the first event
- `eff_rel_y_p50`: the same for the readout y
- `eff_rms_x_rel_scale_p50`: RMS of x_int - x_twin from the first event to the end, divided by s_x; percentiles over pairs
- `effect_end_over_peak_median`: effect norm in x at the end of the first-event window divided by its peak (persistence); median over pairs
- `effect_energy_outside_passive95`: fraction of the pooled intervention-effect energy outside the subspace holding 95 % of the variance of the event-free trajectories (how much interventions excite directions passive activity does not visit)
- `frac_eff_rel_y_below_0.01`: fraction of pairs whose relative readout effect eff_rel_y is below the threshold (near-zero effects)
- `frac_readout_active`: fraction of readout units whose temporal sd exceeds 1e-3 s_y
- `frac_samples_at_floor`: fraction of samples of active units at their lower bound (within 1e-6 s_x of the unit's minimum; rectified units at 0)
- `input_gain_elasticity_x`: slope of log(response RMS during the input) against log(input scale) over event-free trajectories
- `input_gain_elasticity_y`: slope of log(response RMS during the input) against log(input scale) over event-free trajectories
- `kick_clipped_frac_all`: fraction of kick offsets on observed targets (all records) that were clipped at the lower bound
- `kick_rel_scale_p50`: |kick offset| / s_x for single-target first kicks; percentiles
- `latency_y_peak_s_median`: time (s) from the first event's onset to the peak of the readout effect norm within the first-event window; median over pairs
- `n_obs`: number of observed units (columns of x)
- `onset_latency_10pct_s`: time (s) from the input onset until ||x(t) - x(onset)|| first reaches 10 % of its maximum (nominal trajectories)
- `params_param_spread_x_rel_dynamics`: spread across parameter draws: nominal trajectories aligned at the input onset; rel_level = RMS(sd over draws) / RMS(mean over draws); rel_dynamics = RMS(sd) / RMS(mean - its time average); abs_rms in units of x / y
- `params_param_spread_x_rel_level`: spread across parameter draws: nominal trajectories aligned at the input onset; rel_level = RMS(sd over draws) / RMS(mean over draws); rel_dynamics = RMS(sd) / RMS(mean - its time average); abs_rms in units of x / y
- `peak_rate_rel_p50`: percentile across active observed units of each unit's maximum, divided by s_x
- `persistence_ratio_kick`: median network effect decay time after kicks divided by the median decay of the kicked unit's own effect (recurrent persistence)
- `rate_active_rel_p50`: percentile of the samples of active observed units that are above the floor, divided by s_x
- `resp_frac_1pct_any`: response sparsity over single-target first events: the fraction of the OTHER observed units whose max |effect| in the first-event window exceeds 1 % of s_x (_median / _mean / _p90 over events; _any = fraction of events with at least one responding unit)
- `resp_frac_1pct_mean`: response sparsity over single-target first events: the fraction of the OTHER observed units whose max |effect| in the first-event window exceeds 1 % of s_x (_median / _mean / _p90 over events; _any = fraction of events with at least one responding unit)
- `snr_param_x_median`: median over pairs of the effect RMS in x divided by the RMS spread of x across parameter draws (nominal trajectories)
- `snr_param_y_median`: the same for the readout
- `spec_x_f_peak`: Welch spectrum of the active units of x after the onset transient, each unit normalised to unit power, averaged: dominant frequency f_peak (Hz), prominence_log10 (log10 of peak / median density), centroid (Hz), f05 / f50 / f95 (Hz at 5 / 50 / 95 % cumulative power); median over event-free trajectories
- `spec_x_oscillatory_frac`: fraction of event-free trajectories whose unit-averaged normalised power spectrum has a peak >= 10 x its median

## Caveats

- Design dependence: effect sizes, near-zero-effect shares, signal-to-noise ratios, kick sizes, peak rates and the noise floor are set largely by the design (magnitude classes, initial conditions, twin integration). Compare them only with data built by the same design; system-property statistics (spectra, autocorrelation, onset latency, relaxation times, single-trajectory dimensionality, rectification, readout participation) are comparable across designs.
- The public trajectories are deterministic simulations. Twins share their item's integration pieces, so the pre-event difference is exactly 0 and the noise floor is the float32 storage resolution (1.2e-7 of the rate scale); observation noise is a benchmark parameter, not a property of the real systems.
- Initial-condition variability now comes from restarts of nominal trajectories: the share of 'obs.init' records that end in a persistent high-activity state is 0 % in the large systems and 0-11 % in the small ones (init_high_state_frac_x). In version 2 the explicit initial states moved two of the three large systems into that state in about 70 % of the records, which set their rate scales (s_x, s_y) and every statistic normalised by them.
- Current magnitudes are in the simulator's input units; their relation to a unit's threshold is not published, so only their effects are calibrated.
- Single-unit time constants are not published; persistence is estimated from the kicked unit's own decay (self_decay_s).
- effect_energy_outside_passive95 is above 0 in every system (0.43-65 %). In version 2 it was 0 in the four small systems with 3 observed units: with the explicit initial states, their passive 95 % subspace spanned every observed direction.
- Oscillation: every real system oscillates (6.8-17 Hz). The autocorrelation decay is then about a quarter period, not a memory time.
- Ten systems in two classes are few; the recommended ranges widen the real range by a stated rule rather than fitting it.
