A | nonlinear dynamics / system identification | Is the learned latent model actually a state realisation (encoder, closed transition, readout), or does it carry hidden memory? Are the Markov-closure tests (family D, closure gap, Markov rollout consistency) meaningful and correctly implemented? Is the latent-dimension selection sound and generic? Is stability across seeds (G) measured correctly?
B | causal inference | Does the intervention evidence (family C effect error against counterfactual twins, held-out targets and types, latent lifting) justify causal claims? Are latent interventions and lifts legitimate? Which shortcut confounders (time, input, output copy, parameter identity, microscopic memorisation) could produce good scores without causal state, and are they blocked?
C | representation learning | Could the latent space be observationally predictive but mechanistically wrong? Are the baselines strong and fairly tuned? Is capacity controlled (parameter counts, dimension-cheating guard, shared vs independent capacity matching)? Is the selection rule robust to multiple comparisons among many candidates?
D | computational neuroscience | Are the simulations interpreted honestly? Are the low-level interventions (kicks, currents, silencing, edge removal) biologically sensible for rate models? Are the planned claims stronger than the evidence can support (e.g. dataset versions treated as replicates; simulator results called biology)?
E | statistics | Correct experimental units and resampling units? Hidden-data contamination? CIs and paired tests correct (units matched, sums of ratios)? Multiplicity (Holm over the primary family)? Non-inferiority margins? Is the tournament's aggregate ranking statistically defensible?
H | numerical methods | Integration / timing / solver artefacts: recording conventions (pre-kick samples), breakpoints, sampling steps, restart equivalence, event onset alignment, normalisation from training data only, numerical floors in E, any off-by-one between encoders, rollouts and targets.
